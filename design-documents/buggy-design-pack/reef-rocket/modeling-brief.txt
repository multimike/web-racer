REEF ROCKET — MODELING HANDOFF v1.0

PURPOSE
Build the original clean, colorful futuristic buggy. Use turnaround.png for visual reference and ../original-concepts.png for the approved original styling. This package contains concept references, not a finished 3D model or exact CAD blueprints. Rear and hidden surfaces are proposed continuations.

PROPOSED SCALE

Width is maximum static tire-to-tire width; height includes fins, fans and lights. Track is wheel center to wheel center. Overhangs are measured from axle centers to the outer longitudinal extent. Chassis clearance refers to the floor, not the lowest decorative nose tip.

DESIGN AND CONSISTENCY NOTES
1. Silhouette priority: rounded turquoise beach-buggy tub, cream-edged bulbous fenders, coral cage, large rear tires and two high circular fans.
2. Keep the fenders body-mounted. Build a separate hood, lower tub, four fender shells, cream trim strips, and headlight housings.
3. Fan ducts sit side by side behind the seat. Their axes run along the vehicle length; air travels toward the rear. Model open ducts with blade clearance and a separate rotor per fan.
4. Rear deck, small round tail lamps and lower rear protection are proposed completions of the original view. Preserve the visible original wave motifs on both sides.
5. The cream central hood stripe, coral/cream side wave and cream fender edging should be separate decal or material masks, not deep geometry.
6. Allow suspension and steering clearance under rounded front fenders. The sheet is stylized: use the tire diameters and track widths in the spec rather than scaling the entire sheet.

ANIMATION AND ASSEMBLY
- Root at the specified origin; bind chassis to vehicle body control.
- Wheel pivots at axle centers. Keep tire and rim aligned; steering upright parents wheel spin on front corners.
- Pivot suspension arms at inboard attachment points; use look-at/constraints or baked animation for shocks.
- Keep steering wheel, springs, fan rotors and emissive boost meshes separate as applicable.
- Leave fenders and cage on the chassis; test wheel clearance at full steering, bump and droop.
- Export axes depend on the target engine; the coordinate convention here is the modeling master convention.

MODELING ORDER
1. Place four wheel cylinders using the positions and tire sizes above. Block out the floor, seat, nose, cage, and signature rear feature.
2. Check silhouette from front, side, top and the game's chase camera. Fit the intended driver and steering controls.
3. Resolve the approximate image views into one coherent 3D model, following the consistency notes. Do not expect all views to align as pixel-exact blueprints.
4. Build separate panels, suspension, wheels, cockpit and propulsion parts. Mirror structural geometry; keep graphics independently oriented on each side.
5. Add thickness to visible shells, cap tube ends, and use a simple enclosed undertray between chassis rails. Hidden underside plumbing is not prescribed.
6. Add UVs, paint/graphic masks, roughness variation and emissive masks. Keep branding graphics sharp and bold.
7. Test steering and suspension clearance. Set platform-specific mesh, texture, collision and LOD budgets once the game engine and camera distance are known.
